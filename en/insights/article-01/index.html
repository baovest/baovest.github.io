Introduction: Why AI Changes Investment Analysis

  • C.H. Kua
  • AI-Assisted Investment Workflow
  • October 1, 2026
Table of Contents
  1. The problem AI actually solves
  2. Your AI toolkit
  3. The five-step framework

Have you ever looked at a stock and thought, “this could be a great investment,” and then gotten stuck? Too many financial reports, too many competing opinions, no clear place to start. That stuck feeling isn’t a personal failing. It’s a resourcing problem. Professional investment analysis, the kind hedge funds and wealth managers do every day, has always required a team of analysts working for hours, sometimes days, on a single idea.

That’s changing. With AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, an individual investor can now replicate that same professional-grade analysis framework, alone, in a fraction of the time.

The problem AI actually solves

Individual investors face a familiar set of obstacles:

  • Information overload. Too much data, not enough time to make sense of it.
  • No starting point. Not knowing where to look or what actually matters.
  • Emotional whiplash. FOMO on the way up, fear of losing money on the way down.
  • Expensive tooling. Bloomberg terminals and professional research services were never built for a part-time individual investor.

Professional analysts don’t face these obstacles because they have teams and expensive tools. Now, individual investors can have their own AI analyst team instead.

Here’s what AI is genuinely good at in this context:

  • Processing large amounts of information quickly
  • Applying a structured analysis framework consistently
  • Playing devil’s advocate: arguing against your own thesis to remove emotional bias
  • Generating multiple perspectives on the same company

And here’s what it doesn’t replace: your judgment, and the final decision. AI assists; you decide. That division of labor is the backbone of everything in this series.

Your AI toolkit

You don’t need to master every AI tool before starting. This series will show you exactly how and when to use each one. As a preview:

  • ChatGPT: versatile, good at following complex prompts, general-purpose analysis and brainstorming.
  • Claude: excels at deep reasoning and synthesizing complex information; useful for nuanced valuation and risk work.
  • Perplexity: internet-connected and source-cited, ideal for recent news and fact-checking.
  • NotebookLM: Google’s document-grounded research assistant; upload a 10-K or earnings transcript and get cited answers instead of guesses.
  • Grok: real-time access to X (Twitter) and current market data, useful for sentiment and technical checks.

Free tiers exist for all of them, though the paid versions are worth it if you’re doing this seriously.

The five-step framework

Every article in this series maps to one stage of a five-step research process used by professional investment teams:

  1. Screening: find opportunities worth a closer look
  2. Fundamental analysis: understand the business, its financials, and its competitive position
  3. Technical & sentiment check: assess whether the timing is right
  4. Bull/bear debate: challenge your own assumptions before you commit capital
  5. Decision & risk management: size the position, define the exit, and make the call

Each step filters and refines your conviction. Skipping one is how good analysis turns into a bad decision. The point isn’t to find the perfect investment on your first pass, it’s to build a repeatable process that compounds in quality every time you use it.

By the end of this series, you’ll have a complete, reusable framework, plus the actual prompt templates, that takes you from “I think this might be interesting” to “here’s my investment thesis, my position size, and my exit plan.”
